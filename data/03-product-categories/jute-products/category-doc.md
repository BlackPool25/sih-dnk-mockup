# Category Doc — Jute Products (jute bags, shopping totes, sacks, woven fabrics, decorative jute goods)

**Project:** SIH260113 — DNK Export Enablement · **Doc date:** 2026-08-08
**Confidence convention (corpus-wide):** High 80–95% · Moderate 60–80% · Low 40–60% · Speculative <40%. Items marked ⚑ must be re-verified at ship-time.
**Warning banner:** jute is **plant material** — the destination-side rules are (1) **Australia biosecurity (BICON)** for anything that looks raw/unprocessed, and (2) **US Lacey Act** at formal entry. Processed/clean woven goods are low-risk on both — the risk concentrates in *raw fibre, loose seeds, soil-dirty or bark-bearing* items, plus packaging rules (no straw, no second-hand sacks).

---

## 1. HS / CTH classification

Three headings cover the range; **the exact product decides which one — and it changes duty.**

| 6-digit HS | Description (WCO) | Typical in-scope goods | India 8-digit ITC-HS (likely) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5310** | Woven fabrics of jute or of other textile bast fibres of heading 5303 | jute cloth/webbing/loom fabric sold as material, decorative jute panels | **5310 10** (unbleached) → **5310 10 12** (100% jute sacking fabrics), **5310 10 91/92** (jute blends ≥50%); **5310 90** (bleached/dyed/other) ⚑ exact 8-digit varies by source | Moderate–High |
| **6305** | Sacks and bags of a kind used for the packing of goods | jute sacks, grain/coffee sacks, wool sacks, sacking bags (commodity packaging) | **6305 10** (of jute) → **6305 10 01** (jute bagging for raw cotton), **6305 10 40** (jute sacking bags), plus 6305 10 03/05/06/11 series (corn sacks, wool sacks, food-grade bags) | High for 6305 10; ⚑ exact 8-digit per product |
| **4202** | Trunks, suitcases, handbags and similar containers | jute **shopping/tote/hand bags** sold at retail | **4202 22 30** (jute hand bags / shopping bags — confirmed in IJMA GST schedule) | High |

Classification guidance (the duty-relevant decision):
- **Retail shopping tote** → 4202 (higher duty in US/UK). **Commodity packing sack** → 6305 (free/low duty). **Loose woven fabric** → 5310. Some open-top jute totes are claimed under 6305 or Ch 46 (basketwork) in different markets — **the same physical bag can classify differently in India vs US vs UK** (India's GST schedule puts jute hand/shopping bags under 4202 22 30 at 28% GST; the US may treat a braided jute hobo as 4202.22.40 and a simple open sack as 6305). **Build rule: pick the heading from the physical item + destination's HTS**, and expose the classification conflict in the PBE assist.
- Ch 46 (basketwork/wickerwork of plaiting materials) covers tightly-woven/plait-style jute basketry — a secondary code for niche items ⚑.
- **Do not use 5303** (raw jute fibre) unless actually shipping raw fibre — restricted-ish and biosecurity-heavy.

## 2. PBE declaration description guidance

- **Mandatory:** material + construction + use. Correct: `Jute shopping tote bag, woven, cotton-lined, 40x35cm` → 42022230 (India) / 4202.22 (US). `Jute grain sack, 50 kg, new` → 63051040. `Jute fabric, 100% unbleached, 48" wide, running metre` → 53101012.
- **State "new" vs "used"** — used sacks/bags face separate rules (UK tariff splits used/new at different rates; AU destroys second-hand bags/sacks).
- **Declare cleanliness & finish:** "processed, clean, colour/chemical-dyed" reduces biosecurity risk; "unprocessed / with fibre dust / contains seeds" raises it.
- **If it has wood/seed/bamboo handles or trims**, the description must carry them — they pull in the wood/plant-material rules of the woodware doc (AU BICON; US Lacey at formal entry).
- Value/weight discipline as per SOP (Σ pieces ≤ parcel; gross ≤ 110% net).

## 4. Certifications & restrictions

### 4.1 India-side
- **Finished jute goods: export policy Free.** No NOC. (Raw jute is subject to Jute Commissioner controls/trader registration under the Jute and Jute Textiles Control Order 2016 / JPM Act 1987 — **for domestic raw-jute trade, not for finished exports**. Don't ship raw fibre.)
- **Industry bodies (for incentives, not clearance):** **Jute Export Promotion Council (JEPC)/JPDEPC RCMC** needed for FTP benefits (duty drawback, RoDTEP etc. via the DNK rail); **National Jute Board (NJB)** supports exporters (MDA/MAI). Register once.
- Jute is a **natural-fibre textile** — GOTS/Oeko-Tex/geotextile certifications are buyer-driven, not export requirements.

### 4.2 Destination-side — plant-material / biosecurity (CRITICAL for AU; watch US)
- **Australia — BICON biosecurity applies to jute as plant material.** Position from DAFF:
  - **Processed, clean, manufactured jute goods** (woven bags, fabrics, shopping totes) are **low risk** — DAFF's biosecurity risk is managed via BICON conditions (inspection on arrival for contaminants, pests, soil, seeds); typically **no import permit** where the item meets standard conditions. Check BICON for the exact case (search "jute", "plant fibre products", "geotextiles").
  - **Raw/unprocessed jute fibre** is explicitly named by DAFF as unprocessed plant material that may require **processing at an approved arrangement (Class 3.0)** or a **non-standard import permit**.
  - **Packaging rules (apply regardless of product):** **no straw** (prohibited), **no second-hand bags/sacks**, no fruit/veg/meat cartons, no soil — such packing is removed and destroyed with delays/charges (DAFF "Preparing for import").
  - `Plant fibre products and seed handicrafts` BICON case covers specific fibre products — verify if in scope.
- **USA — Lacey Act:** jute is a plant product; a Lacey declaration is required only for **formal entries** (≥ ~$2,500, bonded; entry types 01/02/03/06/07). Small postal parcels = informal/mail entry → **declaration generally NOT required** (same logic as woodware §4.2). **USDA agricultural import requirements for raw plant fibre**: processed/clean jute goods are not APHIS-regulated; **raw/unprocessed jute fibre can require an APHIS import permit** — ship processed goods only.
- **UK/EU:** no phytosanitary requirement for processed jute textiles; no CITES (jute = *Corchorus* spp., not listed).
- **General:** phytosanitary certificates are **not** needed for clean, processed jute goods to US/UK/UAE/AU. Soil is banned everywhere; seed-bearing/loose-fibre items trigger the plant rules above.

### 4.3 What the exporter should carry
Commercial invoice (fibre type + processed/clean statement) · packing list · JEPC/JPDEPC RCMC (for incentives) · for AU: clean goods + compliant packaging (no straw/second-hand sacks) · for US: nothing extra below the formal-entry threshold.

## 5. Shipping lane notes

- **Weight profile: light-bulky.** A jute tote weighs 150–400 g but is voluminous when boxed rigid. Fold flat where possible.
- **Volumetric risk (moderate):** on EMS/Air-Parcel, a rigidly-boxed stack of bags can trip the volumetric divisor (÷4000/5000/6000 — config flag). **Steer ≤ 2 kg jute to ITPS (volume-free, 50-g slabs).** Folded-flat totes pack into small boxes — keep the box tight.
- **Indicative ITPS postage** (gazette Table VIII): 300 g → USA ₹505, UK ₹275, UAE ₹215, AU ₹535; 500 g → USA ₹715, UK ₹425, UAE ₹320, AU ₹800.
- **Cap check:** ITPS 2 kg on US/AU/CA — a 5-bag consignment to the US must stay under 2 kg or move to EMS.
- **Packing:** compress + fold; use synthetic/plastic fillers (allowed) not straw (prohibited on AU); no second-hand sacks as inner packing.
